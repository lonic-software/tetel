# TET-42: what a committed tetel artifact carries, with respect to machine-local absolute paths

All observations behind this memo were taken against the tetel worktree on branch `main` at commit `01b140c74882b17ccb157839cf205794a0aaaa49`. The tracked tree is clean; `git status --porcelain -b` is not empty, but the only entries it lists are three untracked ones, all of them this memo's own outputs — the memo, its `.evidence.jsonl` and its `.tetel/` snapshot. Line numbers, counts and file contents cited below are that tree's; a reader checking them against a different commit should expect drift.

*cites: C1*

## The defect, restated from the artifacts

`render_facts` in `src/compose.rs` documents a contract under the heading "What is deliberately not here": the key is absolute, the label is repo-relative, only the label is rendered, and therefore "a committed document should not carry `/Users/<someone>/...`". Every clause of that sentence is a promise about committed artifacts, and the artifacts refute it. Three committed memos carry 218, 127 and 145 occurrences of a `/Users/` or `/Volumes/` path in their rendered markdown, and 448, 275 and 350 in the snapshot directories beside them. The comment is not describing a mechanism that exists; the same function's body builds the Extent column by mapping each extent entry to `e.label.as_str()`, printing whatever label was stored. TET-42 is therefore first a documentation defect and second a design question, and the two must be settled together — writing a true comment requires deciding what the true contract is.

*cites: C2*

## The decision, per artifact

Three artifacts ship together and they should be held to three different promises, each stated at the width the mechanism actually delivers — plus one guarantee that is not per-artifact at all. A **rendered memo** guarantees that a tool-generated extent label is spelled relative to a repository root when three conditions hold together on the entry: its `world_root` resolved to a repository, its canonical key sits at or under that root, *and* the path as the caller spelled it lies under that same root. That last comparison needs a resolution rule, because for a caller who typed a relative path it has no truth value until one is given — and the rule has to be one a second reader cannot read the other way, which is why it is spelled out rather than gestured at, down to which working directory it means. An absolute spelling is the literal string the caller typed. A relative spelling is the process's *physical* working directory — `std::env::current_dir()`, the getcwd(3) the operating system reports, in which the kernel has already resolved every symlink — and that string concatenated with a single separator between them: nothing is collapsed, no symlink is followed, no filesystem call is made. The condition holds exactly when the resulting string carries the root followed by a separator as a literal prefix. Two choices are being made there and each is defended, in a different place: concatenating rather than normalizing `..` away first, two paragraphs below, and physical rather than logical, in the section on the forgery surface and again in the rejected-alternatives list — where the logical reading is declined as a default and a `PWD`-reading guard is declined for a separate reason, neither of them impossibility. The third condition itself looks redundant and is not; the section on the forgery surface below shows the ordinary symlink that satisfies the first two and defeats the guarantee without it — and also shows the variant of that symlink the condition does *not* catch, which is routed to the fourth promise rather than papered over. Outside those conditions the label is left exactly as the caller spelled it, and the contract stops there rather than promising it is absolute — the next paragraph says why that promise is not one this ticket can keep. Within the guarantee fall plain single-file labels and — by the decision below rather than by an implementer's discretion — a whole-search label's root and the in-repo paths its exclusion note enumerates. It promises nothing about text an author wrote, which means claim propositions *and* fact notes, the latter rendering in the Facts table's own Note column; nothing about a `proc:` command line; and nothing about an entry whose world marker degraded to `no-git-worktree`, which stays absolute even when the file is physically inside the shipping checkout; the committed corpus already contains one such entry, under this worktree's own `.git`. An **evidence ledger** inherits that guarantee for `predicate.extent[]` and explicitly keeps `predicate.world[].root` absolute, because that field is the identity of a tree rather than a citation into one. A **snapshot** keeps every key that is a path absolute — because `key` is what the census predicate compares against `world_root` — and canonical except where `search_key` deliberately adopts the world marker's `--show-toplevel` spelling for a whole-search root; it stores exactly the labels the memo renders. That clause is about path keys and nothing else: a `run` entry's key is `cmdline.clone()`, the command line itself, and `resolve_key` falls back infallibly to the caller's spelling when canonicalization fails on a path that exists. The fourth promise is the document's, not any single artifact's. Clause one is a per-entry rule, and a per-entry rule cannot see two document-level failures: every entry relativized correctly against its own root while the roots are not all the same tree — three shapes now, a repository observed directly, a repository nested beneath the shipping worktree, and a working directory that reached another repository through a symlink — and a label the otherwise branch left relative, which a rendered memo cannot tell from a root-relative one because the marker is a snapshot field and both render sites print the label verbatim. So the contract also requires `check` to report, out of `worldstate::tree_report`, the distinct roots a memo's relative labels are anchored to and any relative label carrying no marker. That is a change to what `check` prints, not to what `render` writes, so no committed memo moves. The rest is deliverable because label and key are already separate fields, the label is built at capture from a `world_root` and a caller spelling both already on the pending entry — the working directory being the one operand a relative spelling needs that is *not* there, and that a fresh `current_dir()` call has to fetch — and both of the sites that render a label, the Facts table and the transplants section's donor extent line, print the stored string verbatim.

*cites: C22, C13, C27, C29, C32*

The half-promise that came out of the contract deserves its own paragraph, because an earlier draft said "and is spelled absolute otherwise" and that sentence was not deliverable by anything this design builds. Nothing between a caller's argv and the stored label absolutizes a relative spelling. Constructed and captured on the release build: with the working directory in no repository at all, `look ../outer/x.txt` mints a key strictly under `outer`'s own root and a `world_root` that is that real repository — two of the three conditions — and stores the label `../outer/x.txt`, anchored to a working directory no reader of the memo has. Run from inside that repository, `look x.txt` stores `x.txt`, which is character-for-character what the root-relative rule would have minted. A third capture makes the point without the caller ever leaving the shipping tree: from inside `outer`, where `link` is a committed symlink to a separate repository, `look link/x.txt` stores exactly `link/x.txt` on an entry whose key and `world_root` are both in that other repository, and it fails the caller-spelling condition under either candidate reading of it. So the "otherwise" branch demonstrably carries relative labels, and the residue that lands there cannot be promised absolute without a rewrite this ticket does not take on. The only absolutization on offer is a join onto the *process's* directory — what `fn resolved` does for the MCP handlers that call it, over a doc comment saying the server cannot know the caller's working directory — so keeping the promise would mean storing a path anchored to a directory the caller never chose and the memo never names, trading one unresolvable label for another. It would also rewrite labels for observations this design otherwise leaves alone, every relative-spelled `look` including the two CLI pin tests, and move their pins for a reason that has nothing to do with root-relativization. So the promise is dropped and the case is carried as a named risk instead, with the marker rather than the shape of the string as what distinguishes a root-relative label from a caller's own relative one — and, since the marker does not render, with `check` naming relative labels that carry none.

*cites: C30, C31*

That leaves the rule itself, and it has to be decided rather than described, because "resolve the caller's spelling against the working directory" has two readings which are not stylistic variants: they give opposite answers on cases this memo relies on. Collapse `..` textually first, and a `look ../otherrepo/x.txt` issued from *inside* the shipping repository resolves to exactly the entry's own key — so all three conditions hold, and the rule mints a bare `x.txt` anchored to the other repository, which resolves in the shipping tree to a different file that exists there, "OUTER x" against the observed "OTHER FILE". That is the failure the third condition was introduced to prevent, reached through the third condition. Concatenate without collapsing, and the same spelling resolves to `<s>/outer/../otherrepo/x.txt`, which does not carry the answering root as a literal prefix, so the entry is refused and the label is left as typed. This design takes the concatenating rule — but not, as an earlier draft argued, because it refuses strictly more. That claim is false, and the counterexample is as ordinary as the case it was meant to cover: with `<s>/c` a symlink pointing back *inside* the repository, `look ../c/f.txt` from `<s>/outer` mints a key under `<s>/outer` whose concatenated spelling `<s>/outer/../c/f.txt` does carry the root as a literal prefix and is accepted, while the collapsed `<s>/c/f.txt` does not and is refused. Each rule accepts entries the other refuses; neither refusal set contains the other, and no argument from conservatism is available. What decides it is which acceptances are the right ones, and the reason concatenation gets them right is the same reason condition three exists at all: it is a test of which tree the caller's own frame of reference was, and only the spelling the caller actually issued carries that. Concatenation preserves it, so it refuses the `..` that leaves the shipping tree for another repository — where the caller was standing in one tree and the answering root is another — and accepts the `..` that leaves and re-enters, where the frame of reference and the answering root are the same tree and the key-derived label `sub/f.txt` opens against that root to exactly the file observed. Collapsing discards the spelling and gets both of those backwards. That is two constructed witnesses and a reason, not a theorem, and it is stated as such. It is also worth saying what condition three is *not* redundant for, because an earlier draft got the boundary wrong: it does not merely catch `..`. Measured on the release build, from `<s>/outer` the spelling `link/x.txt` — relative, with no `..` at all — mints key `<s>/otherrepo/x.txt` while the concatenation is `<s>/outer/link/x.txt`, so condition two passes and condition three refuses. The boundary is narrower still, and it was got wrong a second time: condition three adds nothing over condition two only for a relative spelling carrying neither a `..` component nor a symlink component *and* issued from a working directory lying at or under the answering root. The concatenation stays under the getcwd, not under the root, and `<root>/` is a literal prefix of it only when the root is the getcwd itself or an ancestor of it — which is the configuration every witness of the earlier wording happened to be taken in, the working directory standing at a repository root. Re-derived independently on the release build: from a scratch directory in no repository, the `..`-free, symlink-free spellings `./outer/x.txt`, `OUTER/x.txt` and `./otherrepo/x.txt` each pass condition two and are refused by condition three, and so are `./vendor/v.txt` and `VENDOR/v.txt` issued from a repository root against a nested repository beneath it — the shape where `--show-toplevel` answers with the innermost root and puts that root strictly below the getcwd — while the controls `outer/x.txt`, `outer/./x.txt`, `outer//x.txt`, `vendor/v.txt` and `vendor//v.txt` all pass from the same directories, so the break is a `.` or case-varying component landing at or above the answering root rather than `.` or `//` as such. Refusing at all does cost delivered share in principle, so the cost is measured rather than waved at: across the eight committed snapshots all 489 non-`proc:` extent entries — 82, 13, 12, 95, 43, 58, 79 and 107, against the eight `.md` files `git ls-files docs/design` calls tracked — carry an absolute caller spelling and not one of them contains a `..` component, so no committed entry is classified differently by the two readings, and the delivered figures below — 93, 48 and 75 before the whole-search decision, 206, 99 and 113 after it — hold under either. An earlier draft put that population at 804, because the glob behind it matched nine snapshot directories rather than eight: a memo under authoring has its snapshot on disk and untracked, so a measurement that globs `docs/design/*.tetel/` while the memo is in flight silently counts the memo's own uncommitted evidence as part of the corpus it is describing. The excess is deliberately not restated here as a number, because it has none that stays true — that snapshot grows with every fact this memo mints, the corrections included, so any difference stated is falsified by the act of stating it. The 489 and its per-directory parts are what is stable. The choice is therefore free on today's corpus and load-bearing on tomorrow's.

*cites: C22, C30, C28, C32*

The evidence ledger deserves the arithmetic, because it is the noisiest artifact and the split is clean. Across the eight committed `.evidence.jsonl` files, 5621 machine-local path occurrences sit in `predicate.extent[]` — which `record` builds by cloning each fact extent entry's label, so a capture-time label change fixes all of them without touching `record` at all. Another 901 sit in `predicate.world[].root`, and those stay: a worktree root is what makes two observations comparable, and rewriting it would destroy the comparison the field exists for. Sixteen sit in a grader's free-text `note`. Nothing at all sits in `predicate.source`, `predicate.pin`, `world[].state` or the subject. So the ledger's promise is not "no machine paths" but "the citations are relative and the tree identity is absolute", which is a promise the code can keep.

*cites: C21*

The snapshot's promise is the narrowest of the three and the only one with a hard prohibition in it. Machine-local paths there are concentrated almost entirely in `facts.jsonl` — 448 of 448, 273 of 275 and 348 of 350 occurrences in the three memos measured, with `prose.jsonl`, `targets.jsonl`, `transplants.jsonl`, `counters.json`, `identity.json`, `pending.json` and `refusals.log` carrying none — so the label change reaches them where they live. The prohibition is on `key`: `ExtentEntry::censuses` decides every `target` declaration by asking whether `self.key == self.world_root`, and `search_key` exists solely to make those two tool-captured spellings of one absolute directory agree. A relativized key would silently uncensus every modification target in the corpus. Path keys stay absolute, and the contract says so out loud rather than leaving it as an implementation detail an optimiser might tidy away. It says only that, though. It does not say every key is canonical — `search_key` deliberately returns git's `--show-toplevel` spelling rather than the canonical one when the two name the same directory, which is the reconciliation the census depends on, and `resolve_key` falls back to the caller's spelling when canonicalization fails on a path that exists. And it does not say every key is a path: a `run` entry's key is the command line.

*cites: C3, C6, C22*

The exclusions are stated in the contract rather than hidden in it, because a contract that overclaims is this ticket's own defect at full scale. Author-written text is outside the guarantee, and it is two things rather than one: a claim proposition that names a path in prose stays exactly as written — two memos in this corpus carry one, tet47's C15 and tet56's C1 — and so does a fact note, which is easy to forget because a note is not obviously "prose" and yet renders straight into the Facts table's own Note column. Measured, the notes are much the larger half: 5, 9 and 9 occurrences across the three memos, against 0, 1 and 1 in propositions. What is *not* author-written, and therefore not excluded, is the rest of what sits outside the Facts table: tet56's three donor-extent occurrences are stored labels re-printed by the transplants render site, and the guarantee covers them. `proc:` command lines are outside the guarantee too, for the stronger reason given below. So is any label whose entry's world marker degraded to `no-git-worktree`, even when the file it names sits physically inside the checkout the memo ships in. The consequence is that a committed memo produced under the new contract will still contain machine-local paths, and the contract's job is to say precisely which ones and why — not to promise they are gone, and not to promise a coverage its own exclusions contradict.

*cites: C13, C15, C22, C28*

## Where the path enters, and the one seam that can move

An extent entry carries two spellings of the same observation and they have different jobs. The key is the canonical form of the observed path — `resolve_key` canonicalizes, and falls back to the caller's spelling when canonicalization fails on a path that exists, so the key is absolute in practice rather than by construction. The label is the path exactly as the caller spelled it, plus a line-range suffix, and is what a human reads. `mint` copies both across from the pending buffer field by field and computes neither, so the only place a label can be made repo-relative is where it is first written, in `observe.rs` — and the anchor such a rewrite would need is already sitting on the same pending entry, because `world_root` is resolved from the observed path at capture time. It is worth being honest with the implementer about the size of that edit: `look_path` alone writes the label in three arms of one `match`, the grep path writes its own whole-search, no-match and per-file labels, and the whole-search label gets a rule of its own further down. This is a small set of expressions in one file, not a one-liner.

*cites: C4*

The key cannot follow the label. `ExtentEntry::censuses`, the predicate every `target` declaration is checked against, asks `self.key == self.world_root`; `search_key` exists only to make those two tool-captured spellings of one absolute directory agree. Relativize the key and every census, and the overlap detection that shares the key, stops working. Whatever this design does, it does to labels alone.

*cites: C6*

That seam has a price attached, and it is worth naming before the decision rather than after. The pin is a sha256 over each entry's label, world_root, world_state and output — the key is not in it. So a capture-time label change is by construction a pin change: the same file, opened the same way at the same tree state, pins differently under the new build. Whether that costs anything depends on whether anything recomputes or cross-compares pins, which is settled below rather than assumed.

*cites: C5*

The pin discontinuity turns out to be cheap, and it is worth saying exactly how cheap, because "nothing uses it" is the kind of claim that ages badly. A sweep of every `.rs` file in `src/` and `tests/` for the word finds one computation site — the `sha256_hex` in `mint` — and no second one. `evidence.rs` copies a fact's pin into a recorded evidence statement; `query.rs` prints it; nothing anywhere recomputes it or compares two of them. The only tests that touch a fact pin assert that two pins minted in the same run are equal, and that two taken against different tree states are not; neither hard-codes a pin value, and both drive the CLI with a relative path. So a capture-time label change moves every future pin and breaks nothing. What it does not do is retroactively change a pin already on disk: pins are immutable per fact, so old facts keep old pins and new facts get new ones, and the two are never compared.

*cites: C8, C5*

One naming hazard for whoever implements this: "pin" means two unrelated things here. A fact's pin is the content fingerprint discussed above. A memo's `pin:` is the commit hash an author writes into an evidence-ledger row or into a document-level "at pin `<hash>`" sentence, parsed by `ledger.rs` and `parse.rs`. Only the first moves.

*cites: C9*

## What happens to what is already committed

Nothing happens to them, and that is the design, not an omission. Extent and label are immutable per fact; the eight committed memos keep their absolute labels, keep their pins, and keep checking clean. A reader tells an old artifact from a new one by one added `#[serde(default)]` field on the extent entry, recording that this label was computed root-relative at capture. This is not a new idiom, though the precedent is narrower than the field count suggests. `ExtentEntry` already carries five `#[serde(default)]` fields — `world_root`, `kind`, `pattern`, `out_len` and `matcher` — so the marker would be the sixth. But only three of them say what the marker needs to say: `kind`, `out_len` and `matcher` each document absence as *not recorded*, never *some default kind*, never *zero*, never *some default grammar*. `world_root` reads differently, giving its own empty value a positive meaning — a state resolved from the process's working directory and therefore comparable with nothing — and `pattern` documents a plain default. The marker belongs with the three, and the argument below borrows from those three.

*cites: C7*

Borrowing that idiom means inheriting its preconditions, and both are answerable here. The donor fields insist that absence never be read as a default — "never *zero*", "never *some default kind*" — and an unmarked label genuinely has no default reading. The design's first draft argued this from asymmetry: the CLI accepts a relative path and the pin tests exercise exactly that, while MCP requires an absolute one. The second half is false. `fn resolved` in `mcp.rs` joins a relative path onto the server's own working directory instead of refusing it, and the `look` handler does not call it at all — it forwards the caller's path verbatim. MCP documents absoluteness and enforces nothing. That makes the precondition easier to meet, not harder: absence cannot be read as "absolute" even by appeal to the surface it came in on, and it plainly cannot be read as "relative", so the only true statement about an unmarked label is that it is spelled as whoever captured it typed it, with no resolvability guarantee. The donor fields also insist that absence disable a use and that the remedy be cheap. Here absence forbids reading a label as openable against the worktree root, and the remedy is a fresh `look` under the new build — the same remedy `kind` and `out_len` prescribe, for the same reason: the record cannot be repaired, only re-taken.

*cites: C26, C25*

## How this composes with TET-43

TET-43 is the standing observation that any change to render output reddens every committed memo. The mechanism is `snapshot::check`: it re-renders the snapshot in full and compares the result to the memo's committed bytes, so one changed character anywhere in the render path drifts all of them simultaneously. That is what makes the capture-time/render-time choice the load-bearing one in this design rather than a matter of taste. `render_facts` prints each stored label verbatim; it derives nothing. So a build that changes how labels are *captured* leaves every existing snapshot re-rendering byte-identically, and TET-43 never fires. A build that changes how labels are *rendered* detonates TET-43 by construction, for every memo, on the commit that lands it. This design takes the capture-time route, and its principal justification is that it does not require TET-43 to be solved first.

*cites: C10*

That gives the implementer an acceptance test with a number in it, and the number is not the one a prior investigation reported. This repository holds exactly eight committed rendered memos with snapshots, all under `docs/design/`, and on the build in the tree today every one of them checks at exit 0 with no `provenance-drift` line — verified against a deliberately corrupted copy, so the probe is known to fire. The baseline is 0 of 8, not 3 of 12. The acceptance condition for landing TET-42 is therefore the strict one: all eight must still be clean, with no drift attributable to this change and no pre-existing failure to hide behind.

*cites: C11*

It is worth being precise about what is broken today, because the obvious guess is wrong. A memo, its evidence ledger and its snapshot copied alone into a fresh git repository at a different absolute path, with an empty state home and no workspaces at all, already check at exit 0 with no provenance drift. Citation ids resolve off-workspace. What does not survive the move is a reader's ability to *open* what a fact opened. Measuring that needs a rule, because "distinct in-repo path" is not self-defining and three independent readers counting without one got three different answers: a path token here is the worktree root, then `/`, then a maximal run of `[A-Za-z0-9._/*-]`, with a trailing sentence period trimmed, deduplicated per memo — so the bare root is never a token. Against a clone of this repository at a different absolute path the absolute forms resolve to nothing, while stripping the root resolves 32 of 35 tokens for tet61, 22 of 23 for tet47 and 33 of 36 for tet56. The residue is five items in all and none of them is a relativization failure: `.idea`, `target` and `target/release/tetel` are git-ignored, `.git/COMMIT_EDITMSG` is git's own internal file, and `docs/design/tet*.md` is a literal glob off a command line and not a path at all.

## What the change buys, and what it cannot reach

The case for doing anything at all rests on a measurement, and it has to be the right measurement, counted under a rule that is stated. Counting one occurrence per literal `/Users/` or `/Volumes/` substring, the three memos carry 218, 127 and 145. How many of those are "inside the repository the memo ships in" depends on a second rule: requiring the root to be followed by a separator gives 195, 98 and 119 — 89, 77 and 82 percent — while counting the bare root too gives 218, 120 and 145, or 100, 94 and 100. Neither is an upper bound on what a worktree anchor can reach, and the first draft's claim that it was is refuted by this memo's own whole-search decision, which spells bare roots `.` and so lands on 206 and 99 against a supposed ceiling of 195 and 98. What both figures share is that they classify by text alone, irrespective of whether the entry carrying an occurrence has a `world_root` that resolved, a key under that root, a caller spelling under the same root, or an exemption. Reclassified by this design's own rule, and under the narrower of two predicates that must not be run together: the entries whose key sits **strictly** under a resolved root are the ones rewritten to a **non-empty** root-relative label, and they carry 93 of tet61's 218, 48 of tet47's 127 and 75 of tet56's 145 — 38 to 52 percent. The contract's guarantee is wider, covering a key *at or* strictly under the root, and its "at" arm is exactly the whole-search entry whose key equals the root; that arm yields the empty string under a prefix strip and is disposed of by the whole-search decision below rather than by this count. The two are complementary, not overlapping, and they add: 93+113, 48+51 and 75+38 give 206, 99 and 113. The 93, 48 and 75 are entry counts as well as occurrence counts, but not because every rewritten entry is a single-file label — 4, 3 and 8 of them are searches and no-matches rooted at a subdirectory under the worktree root — rather because, measured, every one of those entries' labels carries exactly one occurrence. That is the number the decision has to be made on, and the gap is not made of paths outside the worktree. Three parts of it are exclusions this design itself writes — `proc:` labels, the one entry whose world marker degraded, and above all the whole-search label, the largest single carrier in two of the three memos. The fourth part, 5, 10 and 13 occurrences, is the arithmetic residue rather than an exclusion, and it is not one kind. Most of it — 5, 10 and 10 — is author-written: fact notes rendering in the Facts table's own Note column, plus a claim proposition in tet47 and another in tet56, and no capture-time change reaches any of it. The remaining 3, all in tet56, are not author-written at all: they are extent labels re-printed outside the Facts table on the transplants section's donor-extent line, which is why a memo can carry more path occurrences than its snapshot carries entries, and a capture-time change rewrites them with their in-table twins. Two occurrences in five is still not cosmetics: a path rewritten from `/Volumes/SSD/Documents/lonic/tetel/src/facts.rs` to `src/facts.rs` is openable by the next reader who clones the repository, which the absolute form is not. But a memo should not spend a containment figure on a mechanism that delivers 38 to 52 percent. Deciding the whole-search label — which the contract does rather than leaving it to the implementer — takes the delivered share to 206 of 218, 99 of 127 and 113 of 145, and that is the number that makes this comfortably worth doing.

*cites: C12, C28, C27*

That is the whole benefit, and it is a portability benefit, not a privacy one. The distinction matters because privacy is the reading a reviewer will reach for on seeing `/Users/<someone>/` disappear, and it would be false. After this change a committed artifact still carries machine-local paths in author-written text — claim propositions and fact notes, the notes alone accounting for 5, 9 and 9 occurrences in the three memos measured and the propositions for a further 0, 1 and 1, so 5, 10 and 10 that no capture-time mechanism reaches — in `proc:` command lines and the keys derived from them, in every extent `key` that is a path, and in `predicate.world[].root`, 901 occurrences of that last field alone across the eight committed evidence ledgers. Anyone wanting a privacy guarantee needs a different ticket and a different mechanism; this one must not be sold as delivering it.

*cites: C24*

The same measurement bounds what the contract may promise, and this is where the ticket's own defect is easiest to repeat. Almost every absolute path in a rendered memo is a tool-generated extent label — but not all of them. Of the three memos measured, 0, 1 and 4 occurrences sit outside the Facts table, and those are of two kinds. Some are extent labels reaching the page through a second render site, the transplants section's "Donor extent (captured)" line at `src/compose.rs:195`, which the false doc comment at `src/compose.rs:252` does not even mention when it says "only the label is rendered". Others are author-typed claim propositions: tet56's C1 opens "The tree this memo's observations were taken against is `/Volumes/SSD/…`", and no label mechanism will ever touch it, because an author wrote it as prose. A contract promising "no machine-local path in a committed document" would therefore assert more than the mechanism can deliver — which is exactly the failure TET-42 exists to fix. That is why the contract stated above is scoped to tool-generated citations.

*cites: C13*

One point about who is at fault, because it decides whether this is a documentation fix or a code change — and the first draft of this memo got it wrong in a way worth recording. It argued that an absolute label is structural: that MCP, through which every agent authors, requires every path-taking parameter to be absolute, so an author who wanted a relative label could not produce one. MCP documents that requirement and does not enforce it. `fn resolved` joins a relative path onto the server's own working directory rather than refusing it, and the `look` handler never calls `resolved` at all; it forwards the caller's spelling straight through. A relative label over MCP is producible, and one was produced. What survives of the argument is the part that matters: the label is the path as the caller spelled it, nothing anywhere derives it from a root, and so the false comment still describes a mechanism nobody built. What does not survive is the stronger claim that the absolute spelling was inevitable. It is a convention with no enforcement behind it — which is why the schema marker below cannot let absence mean "absolute".

*cites: C14*

One class of observation is exempt for a reason that has nothing to do with cost. A `run` records a command line, not a path: its key *is* the argv, and its label is `proc: {cmdline} (exit {exit_code})`. Rewriting any part of that would make the record assert that a command was run which was not run — trading a portability improvement for a falsified record of what executed. `proc:` labels stay verbatim, and a committed memo will keep carrying whatever absolute paths an author put on a command line.

*cites: C15*

## The anchor: `world_root`, not the memo's directory

Relative to what? Two candidates deserve the question. The memo's own directory matches the distribution unit a reader actually clones, which is an honest argument for it. It loses on availability: a capture-time mechanism runs before `render --out` has been called, so the memo has no location yet to be relative to. `world_root` — the observation's own `git rev-parse --show-toplevel` — is already on the pending entry at the moment the label is built, resolved from the observed path rather than from any process's working directory, and that resolution is itself the result of a measurement: attaching the process's directory to every observation made the marker move when a repository nobody had read changed, so the tool asks about the file it opened. The two candidates do not diverge anywhere in the committed corpus: across every committed snapshot, each extent entry's world_root is either this repository or the degraded `no-git-worktree` marker, and no committed memo cites a fact taken against a repository other than the one it ships in. `world_root` wins on availability. It does not win for free, and the ways it fails are worth stating before the risks section rather than in it, because they do not all have the same answer. First, a repository elsewhere on the machine, observed directly. Second, a repository nested beneath the shipping worktree — a submodule, a vendored checkout — because `git rev-parse --show-toplevel` answers with the *innermost* repository: constructed and captured, an observation of `outer/vendor/sub/src/a.rs` derives the label `src/a.rs`, and `src/a.rs` exists in the outer worktree too, holding a different file. Third, and the most ordinary of them, a plain symlink from inside the shipping worktree into a separate repository, because `Session::for_path` canonicalizes the containing directory before asking git — so `look outer/link/x.txt` is attributed to whatever tree `link` points into. The third splits in two on the rule the next section derives, and only one half is closed: the symlink typed into the path is caught, the symlink walked into by the working directory is not, because the rule reads the physical working directory and the kernel has already resolved the symlink away in it. That second half is left open by choice rather than by necessity — a guard reading `PWD` under a validity test against `getcwd()` would refuse it on the CLI, and is declined in the rejected-alternatives list because `PWD` is not the caller's over MCP. So one case of four is closed at capture, and three residues — a repository elsewhere, a nested repository, a symlinked working directory — are what the check-time report on distinct anchoring roots exists to surface. This repository has no submodules and no nested `.git` below its root, so nothing here reaches any of them today.

*cites: C16, C32, C33*

That choice comes with a gap the design must own rather than paper over, and this round it stops being deferred. `src/facts.rs` is only openable if the reader knows which tree it is relative to, and no artifact *declares* that tree. `check` does print the root — on tet61, 21 of its 89 lines contain it when the memo path is given absolutely, and 9 when it is given relatively, because 12 of those lines are check's own `Retrieve them with:` hints echoing the path the caller typed and the other 9 are incidental mentions inside quoted extents on note-outside-extent rows, which exist at all only if that memo happens to have those findings. Neither kind is a row saying "this memo's facts were taken against this root". Beyond that there is only the standing human-owed sentence about working-tree states, which names no root, plus the snapshot's `facts.jsonl` and, by convention, the author-typed pin claim that opens every memo in this corpus. Making the *memo* state its root would be a render change and is therefore TET-43's. Making *`check`* state it is not: `snapshot::check` compares the memo's bytes against a re-render of its snapshot, and check's own printed rows are outside that comparison. So the contract takes it. `worldstate::tree_report` already walks every fact's extent entries collecting `world_root`, and it has exactly one production call site, inside the check driver — its six other callers under `src/` are its own unit tests, in the same file; teaching it to report the distinct roots a memo's relative labels are anchored to, and to name them, answers "relative to what" and "is it one tree" in the same row, at the cost of one function and no drift.

*cites: C17, C10, C29*

## The forgery surface, and why the key alone is not enough

The obvious implementation — strip the repo root off the front of the label — is unsound, and it is worth showing the attack rather than asserting it. Run on the current build: a `look` through a symlink that leaves the repository stores a label reading `<repo>/link/x.txt`, and a `look` spelled `<repo>/../outside/x.txt` stores that. Both labels textually begin with the repository root while naming a file outside it. A prefix strip would turn them into `link/x.txt` and `../outside/x.txt`, the first of which is a relative-looking citation to a file no reader of that repository has. In every one of those cases the key — canonicalized at capture — is the outside path, and the entry's own `world_root` is either `no-git-worktree` or the outside file's own repository, never the one the label appears to sit under.

*cites: C18*

Deriving the label from the key rather than from the caller's string is half the answer, and the first draft of this design stopped there. Under a key-derived rule the two escapes above refuse themselves: the key is not under the repository root, no relativization applies, and the label stays absolute — the honest answer, because the file genuinely is not in the tree the memo ships. But it is only half, and the other half was found by attacking the rule rather than restating it. Change the escape's destination from a directory to *another repository* and the key-derived rule stops refusing. Captured on the release build: `outer` is a repository, `outer/link` is a committed symlink to a separate repository `otherrepo`, and `look outer/link/x.txt` mints key `.../otherrepo/x.txt` with `world_root` `.../otherrepo`, because `Session::for_path` canonicalizes the containing directory before asking git. The key is strictly under that entry's own world_root. So the rule strips and mints `x.txt` — not absolute, not a refusal — and `x.txt` resolves in `outer` to a file that exists there holding "OUTER x" against the observed "OTHER FILE". That is the exact failure this design exists to prevent, reached through the mechanism chosen to prevent it, by an ordinary symlink rather than anything exotic. The fix is not to abandon the key, which still defeats the textual strip, but to require the two derivations to agree: relativize only when the caller's own spelling also lies under the same `world_root` the key is being stripped against. Here it does not — `.../outer/link/x.txt` is not under `.../otherrepo` — so the label stays absolute. In the control, `look outer/README.md`, key, label and world_root all agree and the strip applies. Both operands are on the pending entry already for an absolutely spelled path, so this is a second comparison, not a second mechanism, and it costs nothing on any observation that never left the tree the caller was working in. What it does not close is the case where a caller simply observes a file in another repository directly: there all three conditions hold and the label is relativized correctly against a tree the memo never names. Capture time cannot see that, because capture time does not know which memo this fact will end up in. The check-time report on distinct anchoring roots is where that one is caught — and, as the next paragraph shows, it is not the only one that ends up there.

*cites: C19, C29, C32*

Condition three closes that escape when the symlink is in the path the caller spelled. It does not close it when the symlink is in the working directory, and this is where the design stops rather than adds a fourth condition. Captured on the release build: `cd outer/link` — the same committed symlink, now walked rather than typed — and then `look x.txt` mints key and `world_root` in `otherrepo`, and a bare `x.txt` anchored to `otherrepo`. All three conditions hold, because the working directory the process reads is the resolved `<s>/otherrepo` and the concatenation reproduces the key exactly. The label minted is byte-identical to the one the control capture from inside `outer` mints, and it resolves in the shipping tree to "OUTER x" against the observed "OTHER FILE". Two earlier drafts of this paragraph said no fourth condition could rescue this, on the ground that the untraversed spelling of the directory the caller believed they were standing in exists nowhere in the process. That is false, and the correction matters more than the conclusion it supports. Re-derived in the same fixture: after a shell `cd <s>/outer/link`, an external child process inherits `PWD=<s>/outer/link` — precisely the untraversed spelling — while `getcwd()` reports `<s>/otherrepo`, and `realpath(PWD) == getcwd()` is True, so the spelling is not only present but checkable against the physical directory without trusting the shell. A guard that reads `PWD`, uses it only under that equality, and otherwise falls back to `getcwd()` refuses this capture, because `<s>/outer/link` does not lie under the answering root `<s>/otherrepo`, and accepts the control `cd <s>/outer` unchanged. Everything this memo already measured about `PWD` survives and is what the equality test exists for rather than an argument against the guard: with the `chdir` performed by the process rather than by a shell, `PWD` stayed at `/Volumes/SSD/Documents/lonic/tetel` while `getcwd()` had moved, so the test fails and the guard falls back; under `env -i` `PWD` is absent altogether; and over MCP, `fn resolved`'s own doc says the server cannot know the caller's working directory. So the honest statement is that a partial guard exists and this design declines it, for a reason given in the rejected-alternatives list rather than by appeal to impossibility: `PWD` is the caller's own on the CLI and is the server's over MCP, which is the surface agents author on and therefore the surface on which this hazard is most reachable. The rule names the physical working directory, the case is routed to the fourth promise — the check-time report of distinct anchoring roots, alongside the nested repository — and routing it there is not equivalent to closing it. It is a worse residue than the nested case it joins. A nested repository takes a submodule or a vendored checkout to construct; this takes a `cd` through a symlink. The label it produces is shape-identical to a sound one, so nothing in the rendered memo distinguishes them. And the report is a human-owed row rather than a failure: it says a memo's labels are anchored to more than one root without saying which label is wrong, and where every label in a memo is anchored to one and the same wrong tree it names that root and nothing looks anomalous at all.

*cites: C32, C29, C33*

## The paths that cannot be relativized, and the one that must be decided

Five categories stay absolute, and a committed document should say so where a reader will meet them. A `proc:` label and its key are the command line as it ran, and rewriting them would falsify the record of what executed — the key of a `run` entry is not a path at all. Every extent key that *is* a path stays absolute because the census predicate compares it against `world_root`. `predicate.world[].root` in an evidence ledger stays absolute because it is a tree's identity, not a citation into a tree. Author-written text stays absolute and is two things, not one: every claim proposition, and every fact note — the notes being the larger half, 5, 9 and 9 occurrences across the three memos measured, and the easier half to overlook, because a note does not read as prose and yet renders straight into the Facts table's own Note column. And a label whose entry's world marker degraded to `no-git-worktree` stays absolute even when the file is physically inside the shipping checkout, because the mechanism has no root to anchor against; tet61's snapshot already carries one, a `look` at this worktree's own `.git/COMMIT_EDITMSG`. The honest form of the doc comment `render_facts` should have carried is therefore not "a committed document should not carry `/Users/<someone>/...`" but a sentence naming these five and saying why each is deliberate.

*cites: C15, C6, C21, C13, C22, C28*

The whole-search label is the last case, and it is the one this design was most tempted to call degenerate and hand to the implementer. Measured, it is the opposite of a corner: it carries 113 of tet61's 218 machine-local path occurrences, 51 of tet47's 127 and 38 of tet56's 145 — more than the plain file labels in two of the three memos — and all of them name paths under the very worktree the memo ships in. Whatever gets chosen here decides more of TET-42's delivered benefit than the case the rest of this memo specifies, so it is decided here rather than deferred. Two things make it awkward. `search_key` deliberately stores the worktree root's own spelling as the key of a whole-search record whenever the search root and the worktree root are the same directory — exactly the configuration the census predicate demands — so stripping the root from that key leaves the empty string. And the label carries more than one path: the `no-match (ERE): {pattern} in {root} — {note}` and `search: {root} (…) — {note}` forms interpolate the search root *and* an exclusion note that enumerates every skipped memo path in full, listed rather than counted on purpose, so that two searches over different memo sets of the same size cannot pin identically. The decision: spell a search root that equals `world_root` as `.`, and put each path the exclusion note enumerates through the same rule as any other label — relative to `world_root` when both the key and the caller's spelling lie under it, absolute otherwise. The note's purpose survives, because distinct paths stay distinct under a common prefix strip and therefore still fingerprint differently. The work is contained at the level of the crate: `PendingEntry` is named under `src/` only by its definition, every construction site in `observe.rs`, and the fold in `mint`; `ExtentEntry` by `facts.rs`, `scope.rs`, `worldstate.rs`, `targets.rs` and `checks.rs` and no other file there; `render_facts` only within `compose.rs`, plus one mention in `ledger.rs`; and `tree_report`, the check-side addition, by its definition in `worldstate.rs`, one production call site in `lib.rs`, and six further calls inside `worldstate.rs`'s own `#[cfg(test)]` module — one production caller, which is what bounds the blast radius, and six test callers, which an implementer will have to keep compiling. Outside `src/` the names do occur in committed files that are neither memo prose nor evaluation transcript — a maintenance script's comment, a Python eval case embedding a Rust snippet, a markdown catch log — but none of them compiles, so none is reached by the change. An implementer should not read the `src/` census as a census of every committed mention.

*cites: C27, C20, C28, C29*

## Rejected alternatives

**Relativize at render.** Rejected because `snapshot::check` byte-compares a memo against a full re-render of its snapshot, so this reddens every committed memo on the commit that lands it — TET-43, detonated as a precondition of a ticket that was supposed to compose with it. **Store a separate relative field beside the absolute label.** Rejected for the same reason one step later: two fields means render must choose between them, which is a render change; and the pin hashes the label, so the hashed record would keep the absolute spelling and nothing would improve where it is actually fingerprinted.

*cites: C10, C5*

**Relativize the key as well, for consistency.** Rejected: `ExtentEntry::censuses` decides every modification target by asking `self.key == self.world_root`, and a relative key answers that question wrongly and silently. **Strip the worktree prefix off the label string.** Rejected on a measured attack: a `look` through a symlink out of the repository, or one spelled with `..`, stores a label that textually begins with the repository root while naming a file outside it, and a prefix strip would mint a relative-looking citation to a file no reader of that repository has. **Derive the label from the canonicalized key alone.** Also rejected, and this one was the design's own first answer. It defeats the string strip but not the symlink into a *different* repository, where the key does sit under the entry's own `world_root` and a bare relative label is minted for a file in a tree the memo never names. What is adopted is the conjunction: key-derived, and only when the caller's spelling lies under the same root.

*cites: C6, C18, C19*

**Resolve a relative caller spelling by collapsing `..` first.** Rejected, and it is the reading a reasonable implementer reaches for, which is why it is written down rather than left to be discovered. Collapsing turns `../otherrepo/x.txt`, typed from inside the shipping repository, into the entry's own key under a different repository, so the third condition passes and the bare label is minted — the failure the condition exists to stop, arriving through it. The concatenating rule refuses that one, and is not refusing more in general: it accepts `../c/f.txt` through a symlink back into the repository, which collapsing refuses. What is being bought is not conservatism but the caller's own frame of reference, which only the issued spelling carries. **Read "the process's working directory" as the logical `PWD` in place of the physical one.** Rejected: `PWD` is maintained by shells on `cd` and by nothing else, so a process started any other way inherits whatever its parent last had — measured here as `/Volumes/SSD/Documents/lonic/tetel` for a process standing in `<s>/otherrepo` — under `env -i` it is absent altogether, and over MCP the server's `PWD` is not the caller's at all. The rule names `std::env::current_dir()`. **Add a fourth condition, that the working directory the caller was standing in lie under the answering root, evaluated by reading `PWD` and trusting it only when it canonicalizes to `getcwd()`.** Rejected — and this entry replaces an earlier one that rejected the fourth condition as unevaluable, which was wrong on a measured point. The operand does exist. After a shell `cd <s>/outer/link`, an external child process inherits `PWD=<s>/outer/link`, the spelling with the symlink untraversed, while `getcwd()` is `<s>/otherrepo` and `realpath(PWD) == getcwd()` is True; a guard reading `PWD` under exactly that equality refuses the escaping capture, since `<s>/outer/link` is not under the answering root `<s>/otherrepo`, and accepts the control `cd <s>/outer` unchanged, and falls back harmlessly to `getcwd()` in the stale and `env -i` cases above. So the guard works, partially. It is declined on where it helps rather than on whether it works: `PWD` is the caller's own only when the caller is a shell driving the CLI, and over MCP — through which agents author, and therefore the surface on which a `cd` through a symlink is least under the author's eye — the `PWD` the server reads is the server's. The guard would close the shape precisely where the shape is least reachable and be inert where it is most reachable, which would buy an asymmetry in coverage that the check-time report would then have to explain. All three residual shapes are routed to that report uniformly instead. This is recorded here with its measurement so that a later reader finds a decision rather than an oversight.

*cites: C22, C32, C33*

## Risks and unrun preconditions

Three things this design rests on that were established here, and two it did not run. Established: the drift baseline is 0 of 8 and any post-change measurement is against that, not against a pre-existing failure to hide behind; no code path or test recomputes or cross-compares a fact pin, so the pin discontinuity is free on this build; and off-workspace `check` already passes, so the gain is openability rather than checkability. Three items have left the unrun list since the first draft. The deliverable share was measurable at design time from the committed snapshots and is measured above — 93 of 218, 48 of 127 and 75 of 145 before the whole-search decision. The clone residue was diagnosable and is diagnosed above: git-ignored build and IDE state, one git-internal file, and one glob that was never a path. And the cost of the concatenating resolution rule was measurable without a new build and is measured: no entry in the committed corpus is classified differently by it and by the collapsing reading, because all 489 non-`proc:` extent entries in the eight committed snapshots are absolutely spelled and none contains a `..` component. That figure stood at 804 for two rounds, and the way it was wrong is worth leaving on the page rather than quietly correcting, because the trap is structural: this memo's own snapshot directory sits in `docs/design/` from the first `render --out` onward and is untracked until the memo is committed, so a corpus measurement that globs that directory while the memo is in flight measures nine snapshots and reports eight. `git ls-files` is what distinguishes them, and a memo measuring its own corpus should use it. The general form is worth stating because it is not a lapse of care that more care would have prevented: a memo under authoring is inside its own measurement population, and nothing about being careful removes it — only excluding itself by name does. That is also why the 804's excess is not restated anywhere in this memo as a corrected number. Two rounds ran that correction and each produced a different figure, because the population being counted is this memo's own snapshot and it grows with every fact minted into it, the facts minted to correct it included. A count of one's own evidence is falsified by the act of recording the count. What still needs a build carrying the change: minting fixtures under it and reading which rows still carry a machine-local prefix, which is also how the whole-search spelling and the caller-spelling condition with its concatenating resolution get confirmed against a real capture — the fixture arms above were taken on the current build, which shows what the rule must handle rather than that the rule works; and re-checking all eight committed memos under it to confirm the baseline is undisturbed. The design rests on those two as unrun preconditions and says so rather than asserting their outcomes.

*cites: C11, C8, C23, C28, C30, C32*

Five risks, in decreasing order of how likely they are to bite. The anchor can point at a tree the memo never names, and after this round it is four cases of which one is closed. A symlink out of the shipping worktree into another repository, *spelled* in the path: closed at capture by requiring the caller's spelling and the key to agree on one root. The same symlink *walked into* as the working directory: not closed — and not, as two earlier rounds put it, not closable. A guard reading `PWD` and trusting it only when it canonicalizes to `getcwd()` does refuse exactly this capture, and is declined in the rejected-alternatives list because `PWD` is the caller's own on the CLI and is the server's over MCP, which is where agents author. This is the likeliest of the four to happen by accident, since it needs only a `cd`, and the label it mints is shape-identical to a sound one. A repository nested beneath the shipping worktree, and a repository elsewhere observed directly: also not closed, and for these two capture genuinely has nothing to go on. All three residues are surfaced rather than fixed by the check-time report of the distinct roots a memo's labels are anchored to — a report that tells a reader something is wrong without telling them which label is the wrong one, and that has nothing anomalous to say when every label in a memo is anchored to the same wrong tree. None of the four occurs in this repository today. Second: a caller who spells a path relative and fails the caller-spelling condition keeps a relative label, because this ticket does not absolutize caller spellings, and inside a rendered memo that label is shape-identical to a root-relative one — the marker that tells them apart is a snapshot field and does not render. `check` naming relative labels with no marker is the mitigation, and it is a diagnostic rather than a fix; absolutizing the residue is a separate ticket, with the cost of joining a working directory the caller may never have seen and of moving the pins of observations this design otherwise leaves untouched. Third, a relative label is still not self-describing in the memo itself: the tool will now say the root in `check`, but a reader holding only the committed markdown still depends on the author-typed pin claim, and making the *memo* state its root remains TET-43's. Fourth, the delivered share is 93 of 218, 48 of 127 and 75 of 145 before the whole-search decision and 206 of 218, 99 of 127 and 113 of 145 after it; the containment figures are neither a delivered number nor a ceiling, and 5, 10 and 10 occurrences of author-written text — fact notes and claim propositions — are permanently out of reach of any capture-time mechanism. Fifth, the pin discontinuity is free only against today's readers: any future mechanism that compares a fact's pin across builds would meet a split corpus with no marker on the pin itself to explain it.

*cites: C17, C16, C23, C5, C12, C28, C29, C30, C31, C32, C33*
