# TET-47 — ground what is owed, not everything

This design was built against a pinned worktree, so every file range and command output cited below can be re-opened at that commit and read as this memo read it. One commit landed mid-run — the memo's own first rendering — and a few later observations were taken after it; it touched nothing under `src/` or `tests/`, so the source tree the memo reasons about is the same at both.

*cites: C1*

## Why the floor is the design and the saving is the side effect

The ticket rests on one measurement, and that measurement was replayed here from TET-30's evidence file rather than taken on trust. It reproduces to the record under the ticket's own unit: 125 records over 35 distinct wordings, 90 repeats, of which 56 are the same workspace filing one record per fact and only 34 are genuine cross-workspace regrades. Seven of those 34 gave a verdict no earlier workspace had given, and all seven moved a claim from supported to qualified. Five arrived from the second workspace to read the wording, one from the third, one from the fourth. The seven is a count of regrades, not of records: count once per workspace-and-wording and it is eight, count every record filed by a later workspace and it is ten.

*cites: C22*

Replaying the scheduling rule against that ledger is what shows the floor is load-bearing, but one figure has to be set aside first. That a floor of one retains none of the seven findings is true by construction: a discovery is a verdict no earlier workspace gave, so it cannot occur at the first workspace to read a wording, on this ledger or on any other. The measurement that means something is what happens above one. Seven substantive verdict changes occurred at all; five of them arrived from the second workspace, one from the third, one from the fourth; and capping distinct workspaces per wording costs 35, 51, 60 and 67 gradings at N of one to four, against 69 unbounded. So N=2 buys five of the seven for sixteen gradings, and every further step buys less for more. The floor is not a safeguard bolted onto a cost optimisation — it is the part of the rule that decides how much of what full re-grading was buying survives.

*cites: C23*

## The rule as decided

For a floor N of at least one, a claim is owed when fewer than N distinct non-author workspaces hold a witnessed grading of the wording it carries now. That is the whole predicate. The lower bound is not decoration: at N=0 nothing is ever owed on any memo, the two conditions below stop being entailed, and the fallback for an unattributable snapshot becomes vacuous — so both front ends reject it, the more carefully because the brief command carries only boolean flags today and a numeric argument inherits no validation. The ticket proposed five rules; one is dropped because it has no operand of the right kind, one is deferred for want of yield, and the two that look like separate conditions — never graded, and graded against other words — are theorems of the floor rather than disjuncts beside it. A claim with no records at all, and a claim whose every digested record graded superseded text, both have a confirmation count of zero, and zero is below any floor of at least one. Recognising that is what makes the feature small: there is no set to expose, no id list to add, and `analyze_ledger` does not change.

*cites: C24, C37*

## Where the brief is built today

The ticket's instruction to emit the full brief and a separate list of what to grade is not a matter of taste; it is the only shape the existing code permits without contradicting itself. `brief::build` maps every claim to a brief item with no filter, and its doc comment states in terms that a claim may not be dropped from the brief on account of what its evidence ledger already holds. An owed-scheduling feature that truncated the brief would have to delete that invariant first. Emitting the full brief and an adjacent list of owed ids leaves it intact.

*cites: C2*

The same signature decides where the new computation lives. `build` is handed claims and nothing else — no evidence records, no workspace identities — so owed-ness cannot be decided inside it. The owed list is computed by the caller that already loads the memo's records, and handed to the renderer alongside the items.

*cites: C3*

## Rules 1 and 2 already exist under other names

The first two conditions the ticket lists need no implementation, and that is a better outcome than reusing `analyze_ledger` for them. A claim lands in `ungrounded_claims` only when its record list is empty, and in `out_of_proof` only when nothing it holds grades its current text; in both cases its count of witnessed non-author gradings of the current wording is zero, and zero is below any floor. So both are already owed, by the one condition. The same goes for the case that falls between them — a claim whose records all predate the digest field, which is in neither set because one partition filters those away and the other tests for having no records at all. It too counts zero, because the floor counts exact digest matches. `analyze_ledger` is left alone.

*cites: C4, C37*

What the floor counts still has to be chosen, and `analyze_ledger` shows why the choice matters. It maintains two record sets that differ on exactly the sensitive case: `fresh` requires the digest to equal the current proposition's, while `grading_current` also admits records written before the digest field existed. Grandfathering is right where it lives — treating an undigested record as grading the current text makes the contradiction checks fire rather than fall silent — but a confirmation count is the opposite polarity, and a pre-digest record counted as one of the N would retire a claim from the schedule on evidence that may have graded other words entirely. So the floor counts exact digest matches. It computes that comparison itself rather than consuming either set, which is one more reason nothing in `analyze_ledger` needs to move; what is worth extracting is the digest comparison itself, so the crate states it once.

*cites: C5, C24*

## Rule 3: the pin defect, and why the fix is not a pin

The ticket asks whether the fix for the pin defect is to digest the pin or to compare it separately. Neither, and the reason splits by which record wrote the pin. On the witnessed path — the only one the floor counts — a record's pin is a content fingerprint over an observation's labels, world markers and output, while a claim's pin where it exists is a git short hash lifted from prose; the crate's own fixture ledger is where the operand does exist, both its claims pinned `abc1234`. Those two can never be equal, so the comparison fires on every claim always where a claim pin exists, and on none of the memos this tool renders, where no claim carries one. On the ingested path it is worse rather than better: the pin is copied straight from caller-supplied input with no shape check, so a reporter could type `abc1234` and discharge the comparison by typing — the same defect the floor sidesteps by counting witnessed records only. Under either reading the comparison carries no information about whether a wording has been graded.

*cites: C6*

The channel itself is not what the ticket supposed. Asked to confirm that a claim's pin moves only when its citation list moves, the code says otherwise: nothing derives a claim's pin from the facts it cites. The pin is read out of the prose — a sentence of the form "at pin `<hash>`" sitting between the nearest heading and the ledger table — and the single value found there is stamped onto every claim in that table. That parser is for imported, hand-authored ledgers; it finds nothing in a memo this tool rendered, which is the first half of why rule 3 needed rebuilding.

*cites: C7*

The obvious substitute is a tree-state comparison, and every witnessed record does carry the world markers of the tree its pass stood in. The question is whether a later pass can satisfy it, and the answer is yes — more cleanly than two earlier drafts of this section supposed. A marker is a deterministic function of HEAD and the tracked-tree delta, so at a clean checkout it is simply the commit's own marker; all five memos here carry markers belonging to still-reachable commits, and anyone who checks one out reproduces it exactly. What that reveals is the real objection. Discharging a tree-identity condition requires a pass to move the repository to a named commit before its grading counts, whereas every other input the predicate takes is read from the memo, its evidence file and its snapshot. It is a precondition on where a pass stands, not on what it does. So rule 3 is not a disjunct: not because the divergence cannot be cleared, but because clearing it is not part of grading a claim.

*cites: C29*

The earlier drafts of this section stopped here and reported the question as undecided, on the grounds that a memo under active work has a reproducible marker while one whose repository had moved on never would again. The second half of that was simply wrong: a marker belongs to a commit, and a commit stays reachable. With the false half removed the question is decided rather than open, which is why this section now ends in a decision instead of a stopping rule.

*cites: C29*

Dropping the rule does not mean dropping the defect the ticket found. The divergence is real and it has a home: carry the predicate's world markers into `EvidenceRecord` — one field at read-back, in a type named on twenty-two lines across two modules — and let `check` report which tree each grading pass stood in beside the tree the memo's own facts were taken against. One branch must not be collapsed on the way: the ingested write path sets the marker list to empty unconditionally, with no input that could do otherwise, so an empty set means the tree is unknown and not that it differs. That is the same empty-as-value trap the visibility line has on its identity axis, and it wants the same treatment. Reported this way the observation is a fact a reader weighs, not a unit of work a pass completes by grading, which is exactly what the human-owed partition is for. It took this section three tries to reach a ground that survives, and the two it discarded were both claims of impossibility that this repository's own artifacts disprove.

*cites: C30*

## The confirmation floor, and who counts as a witness

The floor needs a count of distinct non-author workspaces that have graded a wording, and the crate already computes one. `grounding_provenance` takes the claim's witnessed records, dedupes their `pass` values, reads the authoring workspace out of the snapshot's identity, and splits the result into how many of those workspaces authored the memo and how many did not. The second number is rule 4's input. Two things have to change. The count is taken over every record bearing the claim's id with no attention to which wording each graded, and the floor must take it over the records matching the current wording alone. And the restriction to witnessed records has to be part of the rule rather than an accident of where the count came from: an ingested record's pass is a string its reporter typed, so a floor counting those could be discharged by naming alone.

*cites: C9, C24*

It is worth being blunt about what that count guarantees, because the word "independent" invites more than the mechanism delivers. A workspace identity is minted on first use of a name its caller chose, and the crate defines an independent grounding pass as simply a fresh workspace. What a distinct witnessed pass certifies is that separate grounds were captured under a separate identity — a distinct capture effort, not a distinct reader. That is weaker than independence, and it is still the property the replay measured, because the workspaces that produced those seven discoveries did capture their own grounds. The floor should be described as what it counts.

*cites: C33*

There is a state in which the floor has no answer, and the doctrine that refusals are format-level rather than heuristic decides what to do in it. If the memo has no snapshot beside it, or a snapshot without an identity, nothing can say whether a grading workspace was the author's own — the existing report says as much in those two cases in those words. A floor computed anyway would count the author's self-grading as independent confirmation. So in those two states every claim stays owed. That is not a guess about a missing file; it is a decision keyed on whether the file is there and parses, and it errs toward scheduling rather than silent discharge. It does presuppose that the brief path can read the identity at all, which today it cannot, and acquiring that is part of the work the next sections cost out.

*cites: C10*

## The owed list must reach the pass that needs it

A grounding pass is handed a memo path and a workspace name and nothing else, on purpose, so an owed list is only a viable surface if the memo carries what the predicate reads. It carries almost all of it. The `check` driver already assembles the claims from the document, the records from the evidence file beside it, and the authoring identity from the snapshot directory beside that. It opens no authoring workspace. The one input that is not memo-derived is the floor N, which the caller supplies — so two passes given the same memo and different values of N compute different schedules.

*cites: C11*

That divergence is worth naming rather than smoothing over. On the correctness axis it is survivable for a reason specific to what an owed list is: the schedule settles nothing, no value of N marks a claim confirmed, and the full brief goes out unchanged whatever N is. On the discovery axis it is not benign, because the amount of grading scheduled is what decides how many findings a run would still reach. Two obligations follow. The floor in force must be printed by both renderers — cheap in text, not cheap in JSON, where the brief is a bare top-level array with nowhere for a document-level value to sit, so carrying it means wrapping the array in an object and breaking the contract the `--json` test asserts. This design takes that breakage deliberately rather than let the floor be invisible under one output mode. The second obligation is a stated default: both front ends reach the brief today with no floor argument at all, so a caller who supplies nothing must receive a defined value. That value is 2, chosen for the largest saving rather than because it is the only floor that saves — 18 gradings of 69 against 9 at N=3, giving up two of the seven discoveries where N=3 gives up one. Whether that last one is worth nine gradings is a judgement the replay does not make, and a memo whose stakes answer it differently raises N. That is the whole reason the floor is a parameter.

*cites: C28, C31*

## Rule 5: reuse the citation matcher, do not invent one

Rule 5, the textual closure, does not ship — and the road to that conclusion is the most instructive thing in this memo, because it is the same road rule 3 took. The first answer was to reuse `citation_ids_in`, the crate's existing matcher for row-to-row edges, on the grounds that it is already the definition of a citation edge and its documented failure mode is a false positive rather than a missed one. Measurement killed that: across the five rendered memos not one proposition contains a bracketed citation-shaped id, while unbracketed references to a sibling claim do exist in two of them, this memo's own C4 naming C5 among them. The second answer was to match tokens against the ids the document's own ledger defines, which catches those. That died too, on a population the first one serves: the importer's own fixture carries a proposition reading "`foo` returns **4** always [X-2]", and its ids are hyphenated, so a punctuation-delimited token can never equal one.

*cites: C12*

Neither matcher covers both populations, but that is a fact about the two tried here and not an obstacle in the problem — a rule matching a hyphenated id while still finding the corpus's bare references is straightforward to write. What decides the question is yield, and the yield is not merely small but uncountable. One figure holds: no proposition in any rendered memo carries a bracketed citation-shaped id. Bare tokens naming a sibling id resist even being totalled. The count moves with tokenisation; it moves with reading, according to whether an endpoint of "per C3-C5" counts, whether a backticked `ungrounded: C99` counts, and whether "tet28's C9" is a sibling reference or a mention of another memo's claim; and it moves with ordinary editing, as this memo showed by rewording one of its own claims mid-run and dissolving a reference in passing. This design applied a standard to rule 3 — a rule that fires on nothing ever written here is not worth shipping — and it would be dishonest not to turn the same standard on a disjunct carrying a new reverse index, a traversal and borrowed machinery to schedule a set nobody can count twice the same way. The closure is deferred, and what it owes before it returns is a stated yield, not a better matcher.

*cites: C35*

One thing is worth recording about the machinery the closure would have needed, since it was built and then set aside. Check 5 already indexes the reverse citation graph and walks it breadth-first with a visited set seeded by the root, precisely so a citation cycling back terminates instead of looping, and that traversal transfers to a fixpoint over owed-ness cleanly — its two stated preconditions, that a self-citation is not a dependency and that ledger claims are excluded, both turn out not to bind at that destination. None of that is wrong. It simply has nowhere to land while the closure is deferred, which is why this memo carries no transplant.

*cites: C35*

## A schedule is not a termination condition

The ticket suspects that N=2 and a rule to stop after the first round that provokes no revision may together prevent a design loop from terminating on its intended signal, since the first round cannot leave nothing owed. Within this design there is no conflict to have: the owed list is an input to a pass's schedule, no disjunct of the predicate reads it back, and nothing this feature writes records whether it was ever emptied. An owed list still non-empty when a run stops is therefore work declined, not an exit criterion unmet. Whether it also composes with the stopping rules a designer agent operates under is a question about that agent's own definition file, which lies outside this repository and cannot be checked from it — so this memo names it rather than answering it.

*cites: C15*

## Not a flag, and where the parameter goes

The ticket proposes `brief --owed [--confirm N]` and asks whether the owed behaviour should be a flag or the default, weighing it against the argument TET-46 used to reject `--include-tetel`. An earlier draft of this memo answered that there was nothing to preserve because the owed section is purely additive. That was wrong, and the way it is wrong matters. Today's brief is evidence-blind by contract: `build` is handed claims alone, its module doc calls that shape a confidentiality device enforced by shape rather than by care, and a test asserts that a claim already carrying an ingested record must be listed "exactly as if nothing had been recorded yet". An owed list is the first evidence-derived channel into that artifact, so a flag would preserve something real. The decision is still no flag, on TET-46's ground: a flag leaves no trace in what the caller named, only in how they configured the call, and a brief is the one artifact independence rests on. That ground survives only if the parameter cannot become the flag — and `--confirm 0` would produce exactly the empty owed section a switched-off flag produces. Bounding the floor below is therefore part of the no-flag decision rather than a separate piece of input validation.

*cites: C16*

That has a consequence the measurement does not reach, and it is the largest unestablished thing in this design. The replay counts gradings not performed; it says nothing about the gradings that still happen. An owed list is an anchor by construction — whatever it omits is precisely the set of claims that N distinct non-author workspaces have already graded, and since the floor in force is printed beside it, a pass can read that off exactly rather than infer it. Note what the omission does and does not say: the floor asks only that those workspaces graded the wording, not that they agreed, so a claim two passes split over is left off the list on the same terms as one they both supported. A pass is still being told something about every claim it is not asked to grade. And the population most exposed is the one the feature exists to protect: five of the seven measured discoveries came from a second workspace regrading a wording an earlier one had supported. Nothing here establishes that a pass told which claims are already graded grades the rest the same way it would have. That belongs on the ledger as an open precondition, not in a footnote.

*cites: C39*

The floor itself stays a parameter, and the replay is why: N=2 keeps five of the seven findings, N=3 keeps six for nine more gradings, and there is no value at which the trade stops being a trade. That is a judgement about a particular memo's stakes, not a constant. But a parameter is exactly the invisible configuration TET-46 objected to, so the value in force is printed in the brief beside the owed list. A reader of the brief, and a grounder acting on it, can then see which floor produced this schedule rather than having to know how the call was made.

*cites: C16, C23*

## What `check` prints, and what it declines to say

The ticket asks whether being under the floor is a finding at all, given that such a claim is scheduled rather than unsettled. It is not a finding, and the visibility requirement is met without making it one: the count of distinct non-author workspaces that graded the wording a claim carries now becomes part of `grounding_provenance`'s output, printed in the human-owed partition as its lines already are. That placement hides three traps. The function branches by record composition into three states, so a count added only to the branch that already prints a workspace list would be missing from exactly the claims the floor schedules. It branches again on whether the snapshot's identity is known, and where it is not the line must say attribution cannot be determined rather than print a zero. And innermost it matches on how many grading workspaces were the author's and how many were not, with arms for none-by-the-author, none-by-others, and both — but no arm for neither. Restrict those passes to the current wording, as the floor requires, and the empty case falls into the first arm, so the line reads "independently grounded" beside a count of zero and an empty list of workspaces. Restricting the input is therefore only half the fix; the other half is an arm for the empty set that says no workspace has graded this wording. What `check` still does not do is compare that count to N.

*cites: C17*

The blast radius of that extension is small, and the way this memo first got it wrong is worth keeping. By identifier, `grounding_provenance` is named in three files: its definition, field and zero-init in `checks.rs`, one call site in `lib.rs`, one printing loop in `report.rs`. From that this design concluded no test depends on its wording, which is false. Grep the strings the function emits rather than its name and two end-to-end test files appear, asserting on `SELF-GROUNDED` and on `cannot be determined from here`, one of them asserting the latter's absence. They are substring assertions and an appended count would probably survive them, but that is luck, not the census's doing. A census enumerates a symbol's occurrences and is silent about everything that depends on what the symbol produces — its own declared blind spot, and this memo walked into it.

*cites: C18*

## The one mechanism this design copies

### The premises the donor states, answered at the destination

Two smaller corrections belong on the record, because both were things this memo asserted and had taken off it.

*cites: C35*

The first is the floor's marginal price, which this memo asserted falls away and does not. On the replayed ledger, going from two to three costs nine gradings for one further discovery and from three to four costs seven for the last one — so the step that buys the final finding is the cheapest of them, not the dearest.

*cites: C23*

The third is the one that would have been carried across invisibly. Check 5 excludes ledger claims from its graph, on the stated ground that such an id cannot be a cascade root because it has no typed status, and cannot be a row-to-row dependent because it is not a row. Both properties are artefacts of what check 5 roots at. The owed closure roots at owed-ness under rules 1 through 4, which is defined for ledger claims and for nothing else, and every node in its graph is a ledger claim by construction. So the donor's exclusion does not transfer, and the index this design builds covers precisely the population the donor omitted.

*cites: C21*

## What an implementer touches

Seven symbols, each censused across the whole worktree, and the list has moved twice since the first draft — once by losing a symbol and once by gaining one an attack pass reached from source rather than from the text. A new `owed_claims` occurs nowhere in the code, so the name is free. `brief_file` is the big one: its entire signature today is a path and a bool, it loads no evidence, resolves no snapshot and performs no digest comparison, so giving it the owed list makes it a second ledger-analysis driver, and it is called from two front ends. `render_text` gains the owed section, and so must `render_json`, at the cost of its top-level shape. `grounding_provenance` gains the confirmation count. `EvidenceRecord` gains the world markers — but that is only half the reporting line, and the half an earlier draft counted. The other operand is the tree the memo's own facts were taken against, which `tree_report` produces at a call site that runs after `grounding_provenance` has already been assigned, from facts that function cannot see. So the line costs a parameter and a reordering of the check driver, or a new findings field and its own print site. `analyze_ledger` left the list when the predicate collapsed to the floor. `BriefItem` was never on it: its three-field shape is what withholds scope from a grounding pass.

*cites: C19*

## What is measured, and what is only argued

The ticket asks for four pieces to ship together, and it is worth being exact about the standing of each. One is measured: the replay's ordinal distribution and cost curve say what a floor of two buys and at what price. That is not the same as the figure the earlier draft of this memo led with — that a floor of one finds nothing — which follows from how a discovery is defined rather than from anything this ledger did. The closure and the visibility line are argued in this memo from what the code does, and nothing measures what their absence would cost. The fourth, pin-change detection, does not ship as a scheduling rule at all. Extending the measurement's standing to what is only argued would be the easiest way for this design to sound better than it is, so it does not.

*cites: C25*

The exclusion the ticket insists on holds, and more simply than the earlier draft argued. With the closure deferred, every disjunct that ships reads only records filed against a claim's own id, so grounding scheduling needs no edge between claims at all. Nothing here asks an author to re-evaluate a dependent, and nothing here requires that machinery to exist.

*cites: C36*

One condition bounds the whole benefit, and this memo is its own worst case. The count admits only records matching a claim's current digest, so any revision resets that claim to zero and puts it back on the schedule — which is correct, and which means the saving appears only as a ledger stops moving. Run the predicate against this memo in the middle of a round that revised most of its claims and every claim is owed at any floor: the saving is nothing. The replay measured an archived ledger whose wordings had settled. So the feature pays where a design is converging and not where it is churning, and a run's early rounds should expect to grade everything regardless of what the floor is set to.

*cites: C31*

One asymmetry the design cannot close, and should not hide. Every figure in the replay counts gradings not performed, and a grading is not performed only if some pass declines to perform it. Nothing here makes that happen. A brief emits an id, a proposition, an optional pin and a withheld scope per claim, with no instruction prose in either renderer, and the invariant that keeps every claim in the list is the same one this design refused to touch. The owed section is therefore an artifact a pass may act on, not a mechanism that reduces what it grades. The costs on the other side are unconditional, and the largest of them was missing from an earlier draft: `brief_file` today reads a memo and renders it, calling neither `evidence::load` nor `analyze_ledger` and touching neither the snapshot path nor the workspace identity, so giving it the owed list turns it into a second ledger-analysis driver — a change of kind, not a threaded parameter. Add the floor through two front ends, a shape change to the JSON brief, and the widenings in the check path. The saving is real only if passes use the list, and no claim here says they will, because nothing in this repository could check it.

*cites: C34, C38*
