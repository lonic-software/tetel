# TET-47 — ground what is owed, not everything

This design was built against a single pinned worktree, so every file range and command output cited below can be re-opened at that commit and read as this memo read it.

*cites: C1*

## Why the floor is the design and the saving is the side effect

The ticket rests on one measurement, and that measurement was replayed here from TET-30's evidence file rather than taken on trust. It reproduces to the record: 125 records over 35 distinct wordings, 90 repeats, of which 56 are the same workspace filing one record per fact and only 34 are genuine cross-workspace regrades. Seven of those 34 gave a verdict no earlier workspace had given, and all seven moved a claim from supported to qualified. Five arrived from the second workspace to read the wording, one from the third, one from the fourth.

*cites: C22*

Replaying the scheduling rule against that ledger is what shows the floor is load-bearing, but one figure has to be set aside first. That a floor of one retains none of the seven findings is true by construction: a discovery is a verdict no earlier workspace gave, so it cannot occur at the first workspace to read a wording, on this ledger or on any other. The measurement that means something is what happens above one. Seven substantive verdict changes occurred at all; five of them arrived from the second workspace, one from the third, one from the fourth; and capping distinct workspaces per wording costs 35, 51, 60 and 67 gradings at N of one to four, against 69 unbounded. So N=2 buys five of the seven for sixteen gradings, and every further step buys less for more. The floor is not a safeguard bolted onto a cost optimisation — it is the part of the rule that decides how much of what full re-grading was buying survives.

*cites: C23*

## The rule as decided

A claim is owed when nothing has graded it at all, when everything that graded it graded other words, or when fewer than N distinct non-author workspaces have graded the wording it carries now. That is three conditions where the ticket proposed five. The section on the pin rule explains why one of the missing two cannot exist, and the section on the closure why the other is deferred rather than dropped. Of the three that remain, the first two are sets `analyze_ledger` already publishes; only the floor needs something new, and what it needs is for the digest-matched record set that is currently a local inside `analyze_ledger` to be exposed, so that "graded on this wording" keeps one definition in the crate rather than two.

*cites: C24*

## Where the brief is built today

The ticket's instruction to emit the full brief and a separate list of what to grade is not a matter of taste; it is the only shape the existing code permits without contradicting itself. `brief::build` maps every claim to a brief item with no filter, and its doc comment states in terms that a claim may not be dropped from the brief on account of what its evidence ledger already holds. An owed-scheduling feature that truncated the brief would have to delete that invariant first. Emitting the full brief and an adjacent list of owed ids leaves it intact.

*cites: C2*

The same signature decides where the new computation lives. `build` is handed claims and nothing else — no evidence records, no workspace identities — so owed-ness cannot be decided inside it. The owed list is computed by the caller that already loads the memo's records, and handed to the renderer alongside the items.

*cites: C3*

## Rules 1 and 2 already exist under other names

`analyze_ledger` computes both of the first two owed conditions today. A claim with no evidence record at all lands in `ungrounded_claims`, already a list of id and proposition pairs; a claim that has digested records and none matching its current text lands in `out_of_proof`. Neither set catches a claim whose records all predate the digest field, because both partitions are taken after those are filtered away — the same grandfathering the next section turns on. The owed predicate takes the two sets as given rather than restating them, which keeps one definition of "graded on this wording" in the crate instead of two that can drift. The one thing it needs in return is narrow: `out_of_proof` is a list of rendered messages for a reader, so the claim ids have to be reported alongside them.

*cites: C4*

Rule 4 cannot be borrowed as cheaply, and two of its details decide whether it works at all. `analyze_ledger` maintains two record sets that differ on exactly the case the floor is sensitive to: `fresh` requires the digest to equal the current proposition's, while `grading_current` also admits records written before the digest field existed. Grandfathering is right where it lives — treating an undigested record as grading the current text makes the contradiction checks fire rather than fall silent — but a confirmation count is the opposite polarity, and a pre-digest record counted as one of the N would retire a claim from the schedule on evidence that may have graded other words entirely. The floor counts `fresh` only. Since `fresh` is a local rather than a published finding, exposing it is part of the work, and that is preferable to recomputing the comparison in a second place.

*cites: C5, C24*

## Rule 3: the pin defect, and why the fix is not a pin

The ticket asks whether the fix for the pin defect is to digest the pin or to compare it separately. Neither, as it turns out: there is nothing to compare. A fact's pin is a content fingerprint — a sha256 over each observation's label, world markers and output — so two facts minted in one checkout at one commit pin differently whenever their text differs, and it is not a tree identifier at all. A claim's pin is a backticked token lifted from prose, and running the tool's own brief over every memo this project has rendered turns up not one claim that carries one, this memo included. Rule 3 as the ticket states it would compare a value of one kind against a value of another kind that is never present. Shipped as written it is either dead code or, if the absent operand is read as a mismatch, a rule that holds every claim owed forever and gives back the entire saving.

*cites: C6*

The channel itself is not what the ticket supposed. Asked to confirm that a claim's pin moves only when its citation list moves, the code says otherwise: nothing derives a claim's pin from the facts it cites. The pin is read out of the prose — a sentence of the form "at pin `<hash>`" sitting between the nearest heading and the ledger table — and the single value found there is stamped onto every claim in that table. That parser is for imported, hand-authored ledgers; it finds nothing in a memo this tool rendered, which is the first half of why rule 3 needed rebuilding.

*cites: C7*

The obvious substitute is a tree-state comparison, and every witnessed record does carry the world markers of the tree its pass stood in. The question is whether a later pass can ever satisfy it. It can, but only for a while. A marker is a deterministic function of HEAD and the working-tree delta, and recomputing it at the pinned commit with a clean tree reproduces this memo's own facts' marker byte for byte. At the next commit that stops being true, permanently: the authoring markers are frozen in immutable facts and re-rendering carries them forward, so nothing a later pass does can match them again. Four of the five memos in this repository are already there. A condition satisfiable until the next commit and unsatisfiable forever after is not a schedule; it is the red nobody can clear that the crate's own comments name as the defect that forced its stale-record partition to be rebuilt. So rule 3 is not in the owed predicate.

*cites: C29*

Whether it should instead be schedulable is a question this design stops on rather than answers. It has now been decided three ways — a pin comparison, a tree-state schedule, a report — and each reversal came from a real observation, but the last two point in opposite directions and neither is wrong. A memo under active work has a reproducible marker; a memo whose repository has moved on never will again. Choosing a fourth time under no new evidence would be the same process that produced the first three. What would settle it is a measurement nobody here can take: how long a memo's grounding rounds run relative to commits landing on its worktree, across more designs than this repository holds.

*cites: C32*

Dropping the rule does not mean dropping the defect the ticket found. The divergence it names is real, and it has a home: carry the predicate's world markers into `EvidenceRecord` — one field at read-back, in a type named on twenty-two lines across two modules — and let `check` report which tree each grading pass stood in beside the trees the memo's own facts were taken against. Reported, that observation costs nothing and never fails a run; scheduled, it fails every run forever. The distinction between an unclearable truth and a scheduling condition is the whole of what this section learned, and the memo that decided it is a counterexample to both of the rules it tried first.

*cites: C30*

## The confirmation floor, and who counts as a witness

The floor needs a count of distinct non-author workspaces that have graded a wording, and the crate already computes one. `grounding_provenance` takes the claim's witnessed records, dedupes their `pass` values, reads the authoring workspace out of the snapshot's identity, and splits the result into how many of those workspaces authored the memo and how many did not. The second number is rule 4's input. Two things have to change. The count is taken over every record bearing the claim's id with no attention to which wording each graded, and the floor must take it over the records matching the current wording alone. And the restriction to witnessed records has to be part of the rule rather than an accident of where the count came from: an ingested record's pass is a string its reporter typed, so a floor counting those could be discharged by naming alone.

*cites: C9, C24*

It is worth being blunt about what that count guarantees, because the word "independent" invites more than the mechanism delivers. A workspace identity is minted on first use of a name its caller chose, and the crate defines an independent grounding pass as simply a fresh workspace. What a distinct witnessed pass certifies is that separate grounds were captured under a separate identity — a distinct capture effort, not a distinct reader. That is weaker than independence, and it is still the property the replay measured, because the workspaces that produced those seven discoveries did capture their own grounds. The floor should be described as what it counts.

*cites: C33*

There is a state in which the floor has no answer, and the doctrine that refusals are format-level rather than heuristic decides what to do in it. If the memo has no snapshot beside it, or a snapshot without an identity, nothing can say whether a grading workspace was the author's own — the existing report says as much in those two cases in those words. A floor computed anyway would be counting the author's self-grading as independent confirmation. So in those two states every claim stays owed under rule 4. That is not a guess about the missing file; it is a decision keyed on whether the file is there and parses, which is exactly the kind of condition the doctrine permits, and it errs toward scheduling rather than toward silent discharge.

*cites: C10*

## The owed list must reach the pass that needs it

A grounding pass is handed a memo path and a workspace name and nothing else, on purpose, so an owed list is only a viable surface if the memo carries what the predicate reads. It carries almost all of it. The `check` driver already assembles the claims from the document, the records from the evidence file beside it, and the authoring identity from the snapshot directory beside that. It opens no authoring workspace. The one input that is not memo-derived is the floor N, which the caller supplies — so two passes given the same memo and different values of N compute different schedules.

*cites: C11*

That divergence is worth naming rather than smoothing over. On the correctness axis it is survivable for a reason specific to what an owed list is: the schedule settles nothing, no value of N marks a claim confirmed, and the full brief goes out unchanged whatever N is. On the discovery axis it is not benign, because the amount of grading scheduled is what decides how many of the seven findings a run would still reach. Two obligations follow. The floor in force must be printed by both renderers, so a pass's own output records which schedule it acted on rather than the number being printed for whoever just chose it. And the default must be stated: both front ends reach the brief today with no floor argument at all, so a caller who supplies nothing has to receive a defined value. That value is 2, and the reason is a trade rather than a free lunch — 2 is the only floor at which the feature saves materially, and it gives up two of the seven discoveries to do it. A memo whose stakes make that price wrong raises N, which is the whole reason the floor is a parameter.

*cites: C28, C31*

## Rule 5: reuse the citation matcher, do not invent one

Rule 5, the textual closure, does not ship — and the road to that conclusion is the most instructive thing in this memo, because it is the same road rule 3 took. The first answer was to reuse `citation_ids_in`, the crate's existing matcher for row-to-row edges, on the grounds that it is already the definition of a citation edge and its documented failure mode is a false positive rather than a missed one. Measurement killed that: across the five rendered memos not one proposition contains a bracketed citation-shaped id, while unbracketed references to a sibling claim do exist in two of them, this memo's own C4 naming C5 among them. The second answer was to match tokens against the ids the document's own ledger defines, which catches those. That died too, on a population the first one serves: the importer's own fixture carries a proposition reading "`foo` returns **4** always [X-2]", and its ids are hyphenated, so a punctuation-delimited token can never equal one.

*cites: C12*

So neither matcher covers both populations the tool serves, and the yield on either is small: three real edges across every memo this repository has produced. This design applied a standard to rule 3 — a rule that fires on nothing ever written here is not worth shipping — and it would be dishonest not to turn the same standard on a disjunct that carries a new reverse index, a traversal and a transplant of borrowed machinery to schedule three edges. The closure is deferred, and what it owes before it returns is a statement of its yield on a population some matcher actually works on. That leaves the predicate at three disjuncts: never graded, graded against other words, and under the floor.

*cites: C35*

One thing is worth recording about the machinery the closure would have needed, since it was built and then set aside. Check 5 already indexes the reverse citation graph and walks it breadth-first with a visited set seeded by the root, precisely so a citation cycling back terminates instead of looping, and that traversal transfers to a fixpoint over owed-ness cleanly — its two stated preconditions, that a self-citation is not a dependency and that ledger claims are excluded, both turn out not to bind at that destination. None of that is wrong. It simply has nowhere to land while the closure is deferred, which is why this memo carries no transplant.

*cites: C35*

## A schedule is not a termination condition

The ticket suspects that N=2 and the designer's rule to stop after the first round that provokes no revision may together prevent the loop from ever terminating on its intended signal, since the first round cannot leave nothing owed. Those stopping rules live in the designer agent's own definition file, outside this repository and not checkable from it, which is worth saying before leaning on them. Read there, they do not conflict with the floor: none of them makes the emptiness of a list an exit criterion. The loop ends when `check` is machine-clean and no live claim is refuted or unexplained, and the round-count rule keys on whether a round provoked a revision. The same section says in as many words that the human-owed partition is what you hand the reader, not a queue to empty. An owed list still non-empty when the loop stops means the run declined to spend another pass on it — a decision the author is entitled to make and the reader can see — not an obligation left undischarged.

*cites: C15*

## Not a flag, and where the parameter goes

The ticket proposes `brief --owed [--confirm N]` and asks whether the owed behaviour should be a flag or the default, weighing it against the argument TET-46 used to reject `--include-tetel`. That argument transfers, and it decides more than the ticket asked. TET-46 rejected the flag because it would leave no trace in what the caller named, only in how they configured the call. A brief is the artifact independence rests on; two passes running `brief` on the same memo should not receive different documents because one of them typed an extra word. And there is nothing to trade away, because the owed section is additive — the full brief is emitted in either case, so no existing behaviour needs preserving. So `--owed` is not a flag: the brief always carries the owed list.

*cites: C16*

The floor itself stays a parameter, and the replay is why: N=2 keeps five of the seven findings, N=3 keeps six for nine more gradings, and there is no value at which the trade stops being a trade. That is a judgement about a particular memo's stakes, not a constant. But a parameter is exactly the invisible configuration TET-46 objected to, so the value in force is printed in the brief beside the owed list. A reader of the brief, and a grounder acting on it, can then see which floor produced this schedule rather than having to know how the call was made.

*cites: C16, C23*

## What `check` prints, and what it declines to say

The ticket asks whether being under the floor is a finding at all, given that such a claim is scheduled rather than unsettled. It is not a finding, and the visibility requirement is met without making it one: the count of distinct non-author workspaces that graded the wording a claim carries now becomes part of `grounding_provenance`'s output, printed in the human-owed partition as its lines already are. That placement hides two traps rather than one. The function branches by record composition into three states — nothing at all for a claim with no records, a different string with no workspace list when every record is ingested, and only the third carrying the line about records witnessed in workspaces — so a count added to the third alone would be missing from exactly the claims the floor schedules. And inside that third it branches again on whether the snapshot's identity is known, because the non-author count only exists when there is an author to subtract. Where it does not, the line must say attribution cannot be determined here; a zero would assert that no independent pass graded the claim, when what is true is that nothing can tell. What `check` still does not do is compare the count to N: the floor is a scheduling parameter supplied to `brief`, and a comparison printed by `check` would turn a policy setting into a per-claim pronouncement — a document-level verdict assembled one claim at a time.

*cites: C17*

The blast radius of that extension is small, and the way this memo first got it wrong is worth keeping. By identifier, `grounding_provenance` is named in three files: its definition, field and zero-init in `checks.rs`, one call site in `lib.rs`, one printing loop in `report.rs`. From that this design concluded no test depends on its wording, which is false. Grep the strings the function emits rather than its name and two end-to-end test files appear, asserting on `SELF-GROUNDED` and on `cannot be determined from here`, one of them asserting the latter's absence. They are substring assertions and an appended count would probably survive them, but that is luck, not the census's doing. A census enumerates a symbol's occurrences and is silent about everything that depends on what the symbol produces — its own declared blind spot, and this memo walked into it.

*cites: C18*

## The one mechanism this design copies

### The premises the donor states, answered at the destination

Two smaller corrections belong on the record, because both were things this memo asserted and had taken off it.

*cites: C35*

The first is the floor's marginal price, which this memo asserted falls away and does not. On the replayed ledger, going from two to three costs nine gradings for one further discovery and from three to four costs seven for the last one — so the step that buys the final finding is the cheapest of them, not the dearest.

*cites: C23*

The third is the one that would have been carried across invisibly. Check 5 excludes ledger claims from its graph, on the stated ground that such an id cannot be a cascade root because it has no typed status, and cannot be a row-to-row dependent because it is not a row. Both properties are artefacts of what check 5 roots at. The owed closure roots at owed-ness under rules 1 through 4, which is defined for ledger claims and for nothing else, and every node in its graph is a ledger claim by construction. So the donor's exclusion does not transfer, and the index this design builds covers precisely the population the donor omitted.

*cites: C21*

## What an implementer touches

Seven symbols, each censused across the whole worktree, and the list is longer than the argument above would suggest. A new `owed_claims` occurs nowhere today, so the name is free. `render_text` gains the owed section — but so must `render_json`, because `brief` has two renderers selected by a flag and an owed list added to one leaves the other silent. `brief_file` itself must change: its entire signature today is a path and a bool, it loads no evidence at all, and it is called from two front ends, the CLI and the MCP server, both of which the floor has to be threaded through. `analyze_ledger` must publish the digest-matched record set the floor counts, and the ids behind `out_of_proof`, which its single call site makes cheap. `grounding_provenance` gains the confirmation count, and the end-to-end tests that assert on its emitted strings should be run rather than assumed unaffected. `EvidenceRecord` gains the world markers the reporting line needs, and it is named on twenty-two lines across exactly two modules. One symbol is deliberately absent: `BriefItem`. Its three-field shape is what withholds scope from a grounding pass, and no part of this design asks for a fourth field on it — the owed list travels beside the items, not inside them.

*cites: C19*

## What is measured, and what is only argued

The ticket asks for four pieces to ship together, and it is worth being exact about the standing of each. One is measured: the replay's ordinal distribution and cost curve say what a floor of two buys and at what price. That is not the same as the figure the earlier draft of this memo led with — that a floor of one finds nothing — which follows from how a discovery is defined rather than from anything this ledger did. The closure and the visibility line are argued in this memo from what the code does, and nothing measures what their absence would cost. The fourth, pin-change detection, does not ship as a scheduling rule at all. Extending the measurement's standing to what is only argued would be the easiest way for this design to sound better than it is, so it does not.

*cites: C25*

The exclusion the ticket insists on holds, and more simply than the earlier draft argued. With the closure deferred, every disjunct that ships reads only records filed against a claim's own id, so grounding scheduling needs no edge between claims at all. Nothing here asks an author to re-evaluate a dependent, and nothing here requires that machinery to exist.

*cites: C36*

One asymmetry the design cannot close, and should not hide. Every figure in the replay counts gradings not performed, and a grading is not performed only if some pass declines to perform it. Nothing here makes that happen. A brief emits an id, a proposition, an optional pin and a withheld scope per claim, with no instruction prose in either renderer, and the invariant that keeps every claim in the list is the same one this design refused to touch. The owed section is therefore an artifact a pass may act on, not a mechanism that reduces what it grades — while the costs of building it, threading a floor through two front ends and widening three functions and a record type, are unconditional. The saving is real only if passes use the list, and no claim here says they will, because nothing in this repository could check it.

*cites: C34*
